## The sentence

**I still hear the ice-cream cart's bell three streets away, and my bare feet slapping the hot pavement as I ran with coins clutched so tight they left little moons pressed into my palm.**

It is a whole summer afternoon in one breath: the bell, the bare
feet, the hot pavement, the run. The detail that does the work is
the coins pressed so tight they leave little moons in a small palm
— the exact physical memory a childhood actually keeps. Not the
grand event, but the pressure of the coins, the heat of the road,
the bell getting louder with every step.
Every reader who ever ran for that bell knows the feeling before
the sentence finishes. That is why it lands in a head-to-head read:
the nostalgia is not described, it is triggered.
